\section{Innovation}

\subsection{Surprise}

The automatic parking detection workflow is the feature that surprises most testers. By listening to accelerometer, gyroscope, GPS, and Bluetooth signals, the app infers when a car has actually stopped (`ParkingBloc`, `TimerBloc`) and launches a confirmation dialog without the driver touching the screen. Once confirmed, a live Lottie-powered countdown hovers above the map and synchronises with local notifications scheduled. Users react positively to this “it just knows I’ve parked” moment, because the app removes the chore of starting timers manually while still giving them full control to confirm or dismiss detections.


\subsection{Technical Knowledge}

The implementation showcases deep knowledge of information systems across mobile, backend, and geospatial domains. On the backend, Hibernate Spatial queries (e.g. \texttt{ST\_DWithin} in \texttt{ParkingSignRepository}) and database migrations in \texttt{DbPolishRunner} demonstrate familiarity with spatial databases. On the client, BLoC-driven state management coordinates camera, map, and timer flows, while dependency-injected services (\texttt{SignIngestService}, \texttt{SegmentService}, \texttt{TimerService}) keep networking concerns separate from presentation. The Gemini integration applies prompt-engineering best practices—system instructions, retry loops, schema validation—to turn an LLM into a deterministic extractor.


\subsection{Cross-Discplinary}

In developing the parking database, we researched all types of parking signs and incorporated road rules to model typical distances between signs. The app also integrates geographical and GPS algorithms, using the Haversine formula to account for Earth’s curvature when calculating distances, rather than assuming a flat plane. Additionally, the system considers traffic safety laws—drivers are prohibited from interacting with mobile devices while driving—so the gyroscope-based motion detection is only activated once driving has ended. This cross-disciplinary approach ensures both accuracy and compliance with real-world constraints.

\subsection{Impact}

The application has strong potential to improve urban commuting and parking experiences. It primarily targets city commuters navigating complex parking environments, such as Melbourne’s CBD, but its value extends to emerging suburban activity centers like Clayton, Box Hill, Glen Waverley, and Footscray, where the Victorian government is planning to convert them into CBDs.

The app also benefits users at high-demand locations such as train stations, shopping precincts, and sports or leisure centers, where parking competition is intense. Its crowdsourced database adapts to local conditions, providing up-to-date parking intelligence and helping users save time, reduce stress, and make informed decisions across diverse urban and suburban contexts.
